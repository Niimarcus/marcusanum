About Me (&)
My Journey

From financial systems in Haiti and the Dominican Republic to behavioral health and community coordination in Worcester — one throughline: helping people and organizations function better.

'05

B.S. in Accounting

Earned my Bachelor of Science in Accounting from IHECE in Port-au-Prince, laying the foundation for a decade of financial and administrative work with NGOs across Haiti and the Dominican Republic.

'12

Moved to the U.S. for Graduate School

Left Haiti to pursue my Master's at Clark University — adapting to a new country, language of instruction, and academic system while earning a 3.53 GPA. Proof that I can walk into the unfamiliar and perform.

'15

Returned to Haiti — National-Level Leadership

Took on Executive Associate and later Technical Advisor roles supporting Haiti's Ministry of Foreign Affairs and World Vision International — managing budgets, translation, and diplomacy across regional offices.

'22

Internal Controller

Led account reconciliations, procedure compliance, and audit collaboration for Retail Management Group in Port-au-Prince, catching fraud and losses before they became bigger problems.

'23

Relocated to Worcester, MA

Made the U.S. home for good, building an independent interpretation and translation practice in Haitian Creole, French, and Spanish — turning multilingual fluency into a full-time craft.

'24

Housing Search Specialist

Supported migrants exiting shelter sites in finding stable, affordable housing — managing landlord communication, paperwork, and follow-through so families could move forward with confidence.

NOW

Program Coordinator & Behavior Technician

Currently serving as Service Coordinator & Interim Program Coordinator at Seven Hills Foundation and as a certified Behavior Technician at A1 Autism Consultant — holding BT2, CHW, and other certifications in behavioral health and community services.

What makes you effective across so many different settings?
Every role — accounting, diplomacy, interpretation, behavioral health — has required the same core skill: building trust with people and making sure nothing falls through the cracks. That throughline is what I bring to every new setting.
Why the shift from finance to behavioral health and community services?
The work changed, but the mission didn't — I've always gravitated toward roles where I'm helping people and organizations navigate complex systems. Behavioral health just lets me do that more directly, one family at a time.
How do you approach coordinating across multiple agencies and families?
Relationships first, documentation second — but both matter. I stay close to timelines and paperwork so families and partner agencies never have to chase me for an update.
